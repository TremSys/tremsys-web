Objective measurement of movement disorders

Tremsys is a Copenhagen medtech startup building wearable motion sensors and clinical software. We turn how a patient moves into objective, comparable measurements, so clinicians can assess symptoms consistently and follow them over time.

Get in touch How it works

Movement disorders are still assessed by eye

Even tremor, the most common one, is evaluated in a highly subjective way.

Rated by eye

In most clinics, movement symptoms are graded through visual observation and clinical rating scales.

Hard to compare

Ratings can differ between clinicians and between visits, so small changes are easy to miss.

Hard to follow over time

Without objective data, tracking how a patient's symptoms evolve relies on impressions and notes.

From movement to measurement in minutes

Tremsys measures the movement itself, whatever its cause, so it applies across the many conditions that affect how people move. It is built for neurology and movement-disorder clinics, and for clinical research.

  1. 1

    Attach the sensors

    Small motion sensors go on the arms, legs or head, on one side of the body or both. Recording both sides at once lets you compare left and right.

  2. 2

    Run a guided assessment

    The software walks clinician and patient through a standardised protocol, so every recording is comparable across patients, clinicians and visits.

  3. 3

    Get objective results

    Each recording produces measures of intensity, frequency and regularity. Results are kept in the patient's history, with the original data preserved, ready for the next visit.

Where we are and where we're going

  1. Now

    Working prototype

    Wearable sensors and clinical software, starting with tremor, the most common movement disorder.

  2. Next

    Broader assessments and validation

    New assessments covering more movement disorders, and validation of the measurements in clinical settings.

  3. Later

    Secure cloud platform

    Results available to the care team wherever they are, and each patient followed over time, across visits and clinics.

Tremsys is in development and is not yet a certified medical device.
